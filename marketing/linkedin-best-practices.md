# LinkedIn post best practices

Researched September 2026. Sources at the bottom. Applies to a personal profile
posting technical/portfolio work, which is the case here.

## The one metric that matters: dwell time

LinkedIn ranks on how long people stay on a post, not on likes.

| Dwell time | Engagement rate |
|---|---|
| 0–3 s | 1.2% |
| 61+ s | 15.6% |

31–60 seconds of dwell time earns maximum distribution. Everything below follows
from that number.

## Structure

**Hook — first 1–3 lines.** Mobile truncates at roughly 140–210 characters behind
a "see more". No click, no dwell time. The first line has to carry a specific
claim, a number, or a contradiction. Not a preamble.

**Length — 1,300–1,900 characters.** Shorter reads as low-effort and gets
suppressed; longer and completion rates fall. Hard cap is 3,000.

**Line breaks.** One idea per block, blank line between blocks. A wall of text
kills the scroll-stop. Breaks signal new thoughts, not decoration.

**Close with one question.** Comments of 15+ words carry 2.5× the weight of
"Great post!". Ask something a stranger can answer without reading the link.

## Links

Posts with an outbound link lose 40–60% of their initial reach.

Do this instead:

1. Publish the post with no link.
2. Put the link in the first comment, immediately.
3. Add "Link in the first comment." as the last line of the post.

Keep link posts to roughly one in five. Native content builds the reach that
link posts spend.

## Images

| Ratio | Pixels | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 4:5 portrait | 1200 × 1500 | Default. Most mobile feed real estate. |
| 1:1 square | 1200 × 1200 | Safe all-rounder. |
| 1.91:1 landscape | 1200 × 627 | Link previews (Open Graph), sponsored. |

LinkedIn traffic is mobile-first. A 4:5 image pushes the caption down the screen
and slows the scroll, which is dwell time. Use 1.91:1 only where the platform
forces it — the `og:image` on the page itself.

Never post a screenshot with UI chrome in it. Crop from the source asset.

## Formats, ranked by dwell time

1. **Document / PDF carousel** — 2–3× the dwell time of text. 8–15 slides.
2. **Video** — 30–90 s in feed. Under 30 s gets the best completion.
3. **Single image + text** — the baseline. What this post uses.
4. **Text only** — fine, but forfeits the image's share of the screen.

## Hashtags

Use 0–3. Ten or more risks a 30–50% visibility penalty. Pick niche tags
(10K–100K followers) over 1M+ tags, where a post disappears on impact.

## Timing and cadence

The first 60–90 minutes decide distribution. LinkedIn samples a slice of your
network and measures the response before deciding how wide to go. Be available
to reply during that window.

- 2–4 posts per week.
- 24 hours minimum between posts; 36–48 is better.

## Checklist before publishing

- [ ] First line works alone, with a number or a contradiction in it
- [ ] 1,300–1,900 characters
- [ ] Blank line between every idea
- [ ] No link in the body; link ready to paste as comment one
- [ ] Image is 1200 × 1500, cropped from source, no UI chrome
- [ ] 0–3 niche hashtags
- [ ] One question at the end
- [ ] Free for the next 90 minutes to answer comments
